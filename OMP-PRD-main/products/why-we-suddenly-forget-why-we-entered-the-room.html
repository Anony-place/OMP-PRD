YYou enter a room and forget the mission. A familiar word suddenly looks wrong. Your phone seems to vibrate—but it hasn’t. And after a friendly conversation, your mind writes a surprisingly harsh review.

Why do ordinary moments become so strange?

Across 26 everyday mysteries, Suraj Baishya explores the psychology of memory, attention, familiarity, social judgment, decisions, time, and sleep. Discover why recognizing a face is different from retrieving a name, why an unopened notification can interrupt you, and why feeling familiar with an idea is not the same as being able to recall it.

Each chapter begins with a recognizable scene, follows the research, and separates a useful explanation from a story that goes too far. Along the way, you’ll meet earworms, déjà vu, phantom vibrations, the spotlight effect, sensory-specific satiety, sleep inertia, and disappearing dreams.

This is not a collection of brain hacks or a guide to diagnosing yourself. It is an invitation to understand everyday experience more precisely—with clear explanations, gentle humor, optional low-stakes observations, and a referenced research trail.

For anyone who has ever paused and thought, “Why did I just do that?”
