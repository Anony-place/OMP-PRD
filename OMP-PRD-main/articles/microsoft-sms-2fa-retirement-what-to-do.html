Microsoft SMS 2FA Retirement: What to Do Before February 2027

Microsoft is retiring built-in SMS and voice 2FA in Entra ID. The dates that matter, who is affected, and exactly what to do before the deadline.

If you received an email from Microsoft about SMS and voice authentication, or your work IT team suddenly wants everyone to 'set up a passkey', this is what is happening: Microsoft is retiring its built-in SMS and voice two-factor authentication in Entra ID, and making passkeys the default. Here are the dates that matter, who is affected, and exactly what to do before the deadline.

What Microsoft is actually retiring

Microsoft Entra ID - the identity system behind Microsoft 365, Teams, and Azure work and school accounts - has long offered SMS codes and voice calls as a second factor. Microsoft has now officially retired that built-in telephony option, stating plainly that 'SMS and voice are no longer positioned as secure authentication methods' - which is accurate: both are defeated by SIM-swap and call-redirect attacks.

Two important scoping details:

  • This is about work and school (Entra ID) accounts - organisations with Microsoft 365, Teams, or Azure tenants. Your personal Outlook.com or Xbox account is a different system with its own roadmap (where passkeys are also being pushed, but not with this deadline).
  • Microsoft is retiring the built-in SMS delivery, not the concept of SMS. Organisations that must keep SMS for regulatory or operational reasons can plug in their own telecom provider through the Microsoft Security Store (available from October 30, 2026).

The timeline: three dates that matter

DateWhat happensWhat you should do
September 1, 2026Passkeys become the default sign-in experience. Users who had SMS or voice enabled are automatically enabled for passkeys and nudged to register one at MFA sign-in.Register the passkey now, while it is still a gentle nudge.
February 1, 2027Microsoft-provided SMS and voice authentication is retired for everyone except Global Administrators and external users. Anyone whose only MFA method is SMS or voice hits a blocking passkey-registration prompt at sign-in - no opt-out, no skipping.Be on a phishing-resistant method (passkey, Windows Hello, or a FIDO2 security key) before this date - or pick a telephony provider.
July 1, 2027The same retirement lands for Global Administrators and external users.Admins: move yourselves first, not last.

The blocking prompt is the headline: after February 1, a user whose only second factor is SMS will be forced to register a passkey during sign-in to continue. That is a helpdesk event at most organisations, which is why the prep work happens now.

Who is affected - and who is not

  • Affected: anyone using Microsoft-provided SMS codes or voice calls as their MFA for a work/school account. If you got Microsoft's notification email, your tenant has such users.
  • Not affected: users on Microsoft Authenticator push, TOTP codes (including from the Authenticator app), Windows Hello, passkeys, or FIDO2 keys. TOTP verification codes remain supported - though note they are not phishing-resistant, so they will not satisfy policies that require phishing-resistant MFA.
  • Confused by the email but never use SMS for work? If your organisation has no Entra tenant (no Microsoft 365 or Teams business licensing), you can usually ignore the notice - some recipients discovered the email referred to a forgotten free Azure signup.

What to do now - for individuals

  1. Register a passkey on your work account today - it takes minutes and removes the February pressure entirely. Our complete passkey setup guide walks through every device.
  2. Set a TOTP code as your backup factor (Microsoft Authenticator or any authenticator app - see how TOTP works). If you lose your passkey device, this is what saves you from a lockout.
  3. Check your recovery methods - a strong factor with weak recovery is exactly the gap attackers walk through, as our passkey scam guide warns.
  4. Watch for phishing that uses this news. 'Microsoft requires you to set up a passkey - click here' is a perfect phishing pretext. The real prompt appears inside your normal sign-in flow, never in an email.

What to do now - for IT admins and small-business owners

  1. Find your SMS-only users (Authentication methods activity report in Entra) and run a registration campaign before the deadline.
  2. Mind the SSPR trap. The retirement applies to self-service password reset too - and passkeys are not an SSPR recovery method. Users moving from SMS to passkeys still need a separate reset path (Authenticator push, OATH/TOTP, or email OTP). Losing this detail locks people out of password resets.
  3. Decide on the telephony escape hatch - if a department genuinely needs SMS (operational or regulatory reasons), configure a customer-managed telecom provider from the Microsoft Security Store before February 1.
  4. There is a temporary opt-out of the automatic passkey enablement (a Graph API setting, passkeyDynamicMigration) for tenants still mid-transition - use it as a bridge, not a home.

Why Microsoft is doing this

The stated reason is the AI era: as organisations adopt AI agents that sign in on users' behalf, phishable authentication becomes the weakest link at machine speed. Phishing-resistant factors - passkeys, Windows Hello, FIDO2 keys - remove the entire attack class. The FIDO Alliance's 2026 reporting puts roughly 7 billion passkeys in use worldwide, with sign-in success rates around 93% versus 78% for passwords and traditional MFA. Microsoft is not out ahead of the industry here; it is the largest domino falling. Apple, Google, and the password managers have all moved the same direction - FIDO2 vs passkeys explained covers how the standards fit together.

FAQ

Is Microsoft removing SMS two-factor authentication completely?

Microsoft is retiring its own SMS and voice delivery in Entra ID (work/school accounts) on February 1, 2027 for most users, and July 1, 2027 for Global Administrators and external users. Organisations can still use SMS through a third-party telecom provider from the Microsoft Security Store, and personal Microsoft accounts are a separate system.

What happens if I do nothing before February 2027?

If SMS or voice is your only MFA method, you will be forced to register a passkey at your next sign-in - a blocking prompt with no opt-out. Registering early, on your own schedule, is strictly less painful.

Do I still need an authenticator app if I use passkeys?

Keep one. TOTP codes remain supported and double as a backup factor and an SSPR path. Passkeys are the primary; the authenticator app is the seatbelt - see what to use instead of SMS 2FA.

Does this affect my personal Microsoft account?

The dated retirement applies to Entra ID work and school accounts. Personal Microsoft accounts are separately pushing passkeys, but this specific February 2027 deadline is about organisational tenants.
