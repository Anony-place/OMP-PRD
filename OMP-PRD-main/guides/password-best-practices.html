Password Security Best Practices

The ten habits that prevent almost all account takeovers — from unique passwords and managers to 2FA and breach response.

Fundamentals 8 min read Updated 2026-08-23
On this page

  • Use a password manager
  • Enable two-factor authentication
  • Prioritize the crown jewels
  • Respond fast to breaches
  • Everyday hygiene
  • Summary

Most account takeovers are not sophisticated hacks. They are automated, industrial-scale abuse of a handful of human habits. Fixing the habits below removes the vast majority of your risk.

1. One password, one account

When any site is breached, attackers take the leaked email/password pairs and try them on banking, email and social networks — this is called credential stuffing. Reuse means one forgotten forum account can hand over your digital life. Uniqueness is the single highest-impact habit.

2. Use a password manager

Nobody can invent and remember 100 unique random passwords. A password manager generates and stores them behind one strong master passphrase, and autofill refuses to type your password into a look-alike phishing domain — a security feature, not just a convenience. See Password Manager Benefits.

3. Enable two-factor authentication

2FA means a stolen password alone is not enough. Prefer app-based codes or hardware keys over SMS where possible, and store backup codes offline. Details in our 2FA guide.

4. Prioritize the crown jewels

  • Primary email — it resets everything else. Strongest passphrase + best 2FA you can get.
  • Banking and payments.
  • Password manager master password.
  • Cloud storage / Apple / Google / Microsoft account — they hold devices, photos and backups.

5. Respond fast to breaches

If a service you use reports a breach: change that password immediately, change any account where you reused it, sign out other sessions if offered, and watch for phishing that exploits the news cycle. Full walkthrough: What to Do After a Data Breach.

6. Everyday hygiene

  • Keep devices and browsers updated — patches close the holes that steal passwords.
  • Never type passwords from links in messages; navigate to the site yourself.
  • Treat "security questions" as extra passwords — random answers stored in your manager.
  • Do not share passwords by chat, email or sticky notes; use a manager's sharing feature.
  • Log out on shared devices, and never save passwords in browsers you do not control.

Summary

The 80/20: unique passwords in a manager + 2FA on email and banking. Those two habits alone defeat the overwhelming majority of real-world account takeovers.

Put it into practice: run your next password through the local checker — it never leaves your browser.
