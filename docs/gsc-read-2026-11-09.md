## 1. Two corrections to the previous read

**Correction one — "there is no demand" was wrong.** That conclusion came from
the `vercel.app` property alone, which is about a week old and sits at average
position 32. The 90-day `web.app` property shows the same content at position
**9–10** on real commercial queries. The demand was always there; the *new host*
has not inherited the rankings yet.

**Correction two — do not delete the `web.app` property.** The previous
recommendation said to delete it because "it only contains Couldn't-fetch
noise". The 90-day export proves otherwise: it holds the only baseline this
project has, and it is still receiving data through 7 November. It is the
instrument that measures whether the migration is completing. Deleting it now
would destroy the measurement.

What *is* true from the previous read: production canonicalises to `vercel.app`,
`vercel-build.sh` does the rewrite at deploy time, and the `web.app` sitemap
failures are correct behaviour for a 301 host.

## 2. Where the 3 477 impressions actually come from

Three buckets, and only one of them is an audience.

### Bucket A — security scanners, 27 %, not an audience

```
openai_api_key site:web.app            826 impressions  position 3.15
openai_api_key site:web.app * * * *     +62
hacker news site:web.app                28
generator tool site:web.app             15
```

**930 impressions from eight `site:web.app` dorks, zero clicks.** This is the
well-known credential-scanning pattern: people search `openai_api_key site:` plus
a shared-hosting domain to harvest leaked API keys from public pages. Our
`langflow-cve-2026-0768` article is *about* stolen OpenAI keys, so it is the
landing page for that dork:

| Page | Impressions | Position | Clicks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `/articles/langflow-cve-2026-0768-exploited-to-steal-openai-aws-keys` | **1 140** | 3.79 | **0** |

1 140 impressions at position 3.79 with no visible queries whatsoever — every
one of them anonymised. The dork volume (930) accounts for four fifths of it.
**This page is not a content success. It is a magnet for scanners.**

Consequence: the desktop numbers are poisoned. Desktop 2 876 impressions / 8
clicks (0.28 %); mobile 585 impressions / **17 clicks** (2.91 %, position 9.81).
The scanners are desktop, and they explain the gap. **Ignore desktop CTR.**

## 3. The defect this export found: 167 impressions 301 to a 404

`vercel.json` carries a blanket rule:

```json
{ "source": "/guides/:p1(.+)", "destination": "/articles/:p1", "statusCode": 301 }
```

Six old guide slugs have no article behind them, so the 301 lands on a 404:

| Old URL (still indexed) | Impressions | Position | Destination | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `/guides/password-mistakes` | 76 | 37.41 | `/articles/password-mistakes` | **404** |
| `/guides/incident-evidence-preservation-basics` | 68 | **12.54** | `/articles/incident-…` | **404** |
| `/guides/secure-backup-recovery-plan` | 8 | 28.38 | `/articles/secure-…` | **404** |
| `/guides/database-encryption-strategies` | 7 | 41.29 | `/articles/database-…` | **404** |
| `/guides/password-mistakes/` (trailing slash) | 4 | 10.00 | — | **404** |
| `/guides/wireless-network-cryptography` | 2 | 9.00 | `/articles/wireless-…` | **404** |
| `/guides/devsecops-integration-rules` | 1 | 28.00 | `/articles/devsecops-…` | **404** |
| `/guides/secure-backup-recovery-plan.html` | 1 | 42.00 | — | **404** |
| **total** | **167** | | | |

`/guides/incident-evidence-preservation-basics` sits at **position 12.54** — the
second-best position anywhere in either export — and it is a 404.

**This is not only lost traffic, it is the thing blocking the migration.** Google
does not transfer authority from a 301 whose destination returns 404. Every one
of these eight URLs keeps the old host's index entry alive and keeps
consolidation incomplete. Fixing them does two jobs at once.

## 5. The batch, in order

**Step 1 — repair the eight redirects** (`vercel.json`, before the blanket
rule). Every target is a live page that already exists:

| From | To | Why this target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/guides/password-mistakes` + trailing slash | `/articles/common-password-mistakes` | Same topic, live |
| `/guides/incident-evidence-preservation-basics` | `/cheatsheets/incident-response-first-hour-cheatsheet` | Same job — preserve evidence in hour one |
| `/guides/secure-backup-recovery-plan` + `.html` | `/articles/after-a-data-breach` | Recovery after loss |
| `/guides/database-encryption-strategies` | `/articles/common-cryptography-failures` | Encryption, honest framing |
| `/guides/wireless-network-cryptography` | `/articles/common-cryptography-failures` | Only live cryptography page |
| `/guides/devsecops-integration-rules` | `/security-checklist` | Closest live asset |

**Step 2 — operator: Change of Address `web.app` → `vercel.app`.** Not delete.
That is the signal Google uses to move a host, and it is the only way the
homepage's 22 clicks arrive on the property we are building.

**Step 3 — internal links to the tools.** `/checker` ranks at 3.14 and receives
7 impressions in 90 days. The tools are the only thing on the site that can
convert, and nothing points at them.

**Step 4 — the error-string content engine**, unchanged and still the best bet
for new demand: ten-variant autocomplete tails on
`bitwarden autofill not working`, `burp suite browser not launching`,
`metasploit database not connected`.

**Stop rule unchanged:** three pages under this rule failing to reach 10
impressions in 14 days falsifies it.
